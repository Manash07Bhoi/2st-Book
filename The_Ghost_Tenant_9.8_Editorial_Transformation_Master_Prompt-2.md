## 5. Non-negotiable editorial rules

### 5.1 No fabricated material

- Never invent facts about Mumbai, Girgaum, Maharashtra tenancy law, redevelopment procedure, police powers, court procedure, notarisation, forensic handwriting examination or any named institution.
- Never invent a source, legal citation, case, quotation, expert opinion, statistic, reader review, award, sales figure or publisher endorsement.
- Never insert dummy text, placeholder paragraphs, fake evidence, fabricated dates, invented case numbers, fake procedural details or unsupported claims.
- Never pretend a legal, cultural or historical question has been verified when it has not.
- When evidence is unavailable, mark the issue as **UNVERIFIED** in the separate research register and propose a conservative narrative treatment.

### 5.2 Preserve the author's voice

- Do not flatten the prose into generic AI-style smoothness.
- Do not replace distinctive Indian English with American English.
- Do not erase Marathi or local terms merely because they are unfamiliar; verify spelling, meaning and context instead.
- Do not force every paragraph to be lyrical.
- Do not give every character identical eloquence, metaphor habits or philosophical insight.
- Do not add decorative metaphors to make prose appear sophisticated.
- Retain the author's characteristic attention to ordinary objects, routine, memory and place where it is doing real narrative work.
- When editing a strong passage, change as little as possible.

### 5.3 No plot changes without a reason

- Do not change the protagonist's identity, essential relationships, moral centre, central fraud, key motifs or thematic destination merely to make the plot more commercial.
- Do not invent a new villain, secret relative, surprise death, romance, conspiracy layer or action sequence unless the existing story genuinely requires it and the change is documented for approval.
- Do not remove a scene solely because it is quiet. Quiet scenes may carry character, atmosphere or evidence.
- Do not retain a scene solely because it contains beautiful prose. It must also perform a narrative function.
- Do not change a legal outcome, date, sum, signature, document or witness fact without tracing the consequences throughout the manuscript.

### 5.4 Do not game the score

- Never set every category to 9.8 or 10 to satisfy the target.
- Never hide weaknesses in the final report.
- Never equate longer, more ornate or more dramatic prose with better prose.
- Never claim that the novel is objectively a 9.8/10.
- Provide evidence for every major rating and identify remaining limitations.
- A lower honest score is more useful than a manufactured high score.

### PHASE 3 — Story architecture and chapter-level developmental edit

**Objective:** improve the reader's experience across the complete novel while preserving its identity.

#### 3.1 Opening and reader contract

Evaluate whether the prologue and first chapters establish:

- Shanta's emotional history and relationship to the room;
- the key/nail motif without overexplaining it;
- the threat of redevelopment/documentary manipulation;
- a compelling reader question;
- a clear and believable transition into the lock/occupancy crisis;
- the correct balance of context and forward movement.

Do not explain the entire conspiracy too early. Do not withhold basic orientation so long that readers become confused.

#### 3.2 Escalation

Check that each major development changes the pressure, available choices or reader understanding. Build an escalation ladder from the initial lock change through the documentary investigation, resistance, community involvement, evidence collection, setbacks, court proceedings and final restoration.

A chapter should not merely repeat that documents matter. It should reveal something new, change a relationship, force a decision, increase risk, impose a cost or transform the reader's interpretation.

#### 3.3 Chapter and scene necessity

For each scene, ask:

- What is different by the end?
- What would be lost if it were removed?
- Is the information already known?
- Does it create a choice or consequence?
- Is the scene in the most effective position?
- Does its length match its dramatic importance?
- Is the emotional beat earned by preceding action?

Merge, shorten, move or remove material only when the case is clear. Preserve scenes that provide necessary quiet, domestic texture or human contrast.

#### 3.4 Tension and uncertainty

The novel is not required to become an action thriller. Build tension through:

- uncertainty about who created and authorised the documents;
- the competing interests of Shanta and Suresh;
- the risks of a false signature and a believable-looking record;
- pressure on family loyalty and trust;
- the cost of speaking, testifying or cooperating;
- time, fatigue, money and procedural delay;
- evidence that appears promising but proves incomplete;
- the difference between civil restoration and criminal accountability.

Do not manufacture cliffhangers at every chapter ending. Use quiet dread, reversals, discoveries and consequences.

#### 3.5 The middle section

Pay special attention to Chapters 8–35, without assuming that every chapter in that range is weak. Identify the actual repetitive or slow sequences. Look for multiple scenes performing the same explanatory task, repeated restatements of the evidence, long stretches in which the protagonist only receives information, and supporting characters who exist only to deliver a thematic statement.

Use targeted compression rather than indiscriminate cutting. Preserve the procedural texture that distinguishes the book.

### PHASE 7 — Line edit: prose, rhythm, clarity and restraint

**Objective:** make the prose more precise and affecting without erasing the author's voice.

Assess each paragraph for:

- clarity of meaning;
- sentence rhythm and variation;
- concrete sensory detail;
- image freshness;
- precision of verbs and nouns;
- unnecessary modifiers;
- repeated information;
- metaphor density;
- emotional overstatement;
- explanatory narration after a scene has already demonstrated the point;
- abstract statements that could be grounded in action;
- awkward or ambiguous pronouns;
- unintentional shifts in narrative distance;
- excessive filter phrases or filler;
- repeated rhythms and syntactic patterns.

#### 7.1 Metaphor and aphorism audit

The novel's literary style frequently gives ordinary objects and systems metaphorical weight. This is an asset when selective. It weakens when every object, building sound, document or exchange becomes an overt symbol.

Create a register of the strongest recurring images and aphoristic lines. Protect the best ones. For each other candidate, ask:

1. Is the image original or familiar?
2. Does it reveal character, setting or meaning?
3. Is the image consistent with the speaker/narrator?
4. Has a similar image appeared nearby?
5. Does it interrupt the scene's natural rhythm?
6. Would a plain sentence be stronger?

Do not remove distinctive style in the name of minimalism. Do not retain every metaphor in the name of voice.

#### 7.2 Indian English and language consistency

Create a style sheet before copyediting. Establish consistent treatment of:

- Indian/British English spelling and vocabulary;
- punctuation and quotation marks;
- dialogue paragraphing;
- italics and emphasis;
- Marathi/Hindi/local terms and transliteration;
- honorifics and forms of address;
- dates, times, rupees and lakh/crore expressions;
- abbreviations and legal terms;
- numerals, room numbers and document references;
- hyphenation and compounds;
- capitalization of institutions, offices and defined documents.

Do not convert culturally specific phrasing into generic global prose unless it is genuinely unclear.

**Pass B — Proofread:** inspect the final composed text page by page, preferably after typesetting, for remaining errors, awkward breaks, missing text and layout defects.

Specific high-priority checks already observed in the supplied reader PDF include:

## 7. Editorial scoring rubric

Use this rubric twice: once for the baseline and once for the final version. Score each category from 0 to 10, then calculate the weighted score. The weights are an internal diagnostic instrument, not an industry-standard formula.

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Premise, identity and reader promise | 7% |
| Story architecture and causality | 12% |
| Character depth, agency and arcs | 12% |
| Conflict, suspense and revelation | 10% |
| Pacing and chapter effectiveness | 8% |
| Prose, style, clarity and restraint | 10% |
| Dialogue and individual voice | 8% |
| Setting and cultural specificity | 6% |
| Emotional credibility and resonance | 7% |
| Themes and motif development | 6% |
| Ending and payoff | 7% |
| Legal/procedural plausibility | 4% |
| Copyediting and language correctness | 2% |
| Production and edition quality | 1% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

### Score discipline

- A 10 means exceptional execution with no material weakness in that category; it does not mean the editor likes it.
- A 9 means excellent with only small, non-disruptive limitations.
- An 8 means strong but with identifiable weaknesses.
- A 7 means competent, with material room for improvement.
- A 6 or lower indicates a weakness that noticeably harms the reading experience.
- Give a short evidence-based justification for every category.
- State confidence in the score and any limitations of the review.
- Do not round up merely to reach 9.8.
- If the final result is 9.1, 9.4 or another honest number, report it. Explain what prevents a higher rating.

A strong score is not proof of commercial success, critical acclaim, sales, awards or reader consensus. Do not make those claims.

# PART IV — SPECIALISED QUALITY TESTS

## 24. The first-30-pages test

Read the first thirty pages as if you know nothing about the book.

Can a new reader answer:

- Who is Shanta?
- What does the room mean to her?
- What is the immediate disturbance?
- What has been taken or changed?
- Who is the person inside the room?
- Why is the paperwork suspicious?
- What does Shanta intend to do next?
- What question makes the reader continue?

Do not answer these questions by inserting a summary paragraph. The scenes should make the essential information clear naturally.

## 25. The sceptical-evidence test

Imagine a reader who distrusts every convenient coincidence. Ask:

- Could this document really exist in this form?
- Who had access to the original?
- How could a false signature pass the earlier checks?
- Why did no one identify the problem sooner?
- What would each party gain or risk?
- How does the protagonist obtain each piece of evidence?
- Is the evidence admissible/relevant in the way the dialogue suggests?
- Does a later discovery depend on something planted earlier?
- Are the witnesses independent, or are they repeating one another?
- Does the final judgment rely on information already established?

Any unexplained coincidence must be examined. Do not solve every gap by adding another secret document.

## 26. The character-voice test

Remove dialogue tags from selected scenes and see whether the speakers remain distinguishable. If not, strengthen voice through vocabulary, syntax, priorities, humour, education, temperament and what each person chooses not to say.

Do not create caricatured accents or phonetic spellings to force differentiation. Voice comes primarily from thought, rhythm, attention and intention.

## 27. The metaphor-restraint test

For every page with multiple conspicuous metaphors or aphoristic statements, ask whether they compete. Retain the most effective images and allow surrounding prose to carry the scene plainly. The novel should not sound as if it is continuously auditioning for quotation collections.

## 28. The emotional-cost test

For every victory, ask what it costs and what remains damaged. For every loss, ask whether its emotional consequence persists in later behaviour. Do not let legal victory erase fatigue, bereavement, shame, distrust, financial injury or lost time.

## 29. The ending test

After the final sentence, a reader should understand what happened to the room, what changed in Shanta, what the building learned, what remains unresolved and why the key/nail image matters. The reader should not need an explanatory essay to understand the ending.

## 30. The publication-file test

Open the generated reader and print PDFs on more than one renderer/device where possible. A file that exports successfully is not necessarily a file that is publication-ready. Confirm visual legibility, text integrity, page sequence, metadata and final-source correspondence.

# PART V — EDITORIAL DECISION RULES

## 31. How to decide whether to change a passage

Use this order of priority:

1. **Truth and continuity:** Is it factually/chronologically consistent with the novel's established world?
2. **Comprehension:** Can a careful reader understand it on first reading?
3. **Character:** Does it fit the person speaking or acting?
4. **Causality:** Does it make the next event believable?
5. **Scene function:** Does it change something that matters?
6. **Rhythm and style:** Is the sentence effective and distinctive?
7. **Polish:** Are grammar, punctuation and presentation correct?

Do not polish a sentence whose underlying scene is structurally unnecessary until the scene decision is made.

## 32. Least-invasive-edit principle

Choose the smallest intervention that fully resolves the problem. Use the following ladder:

1. Correct a typo or punctuation error.
2. Clarify a sentence.
3. Remove repeated explanation.
4. Adjust a paragraph or dialogue beat.
5. Rework a scene while preserving its function.
6. Move or combine scenes.
7. Restructure a chapter sequence.
8. Change a major plot mechanism or character arc only when essential and explicitly documented.

The greater the intervention, the stronger the evidence and documentation required.

## 33. When not to change something

Do not revise a passage merely because:

- another wording sounds more fashionable;
- a generic writing guide recommends shorter sentences;
- a metaphor is unusual but clear and appropriate;
- a quiet scene lacks overt action but changes emotional understanding;
- a character behaves imperfectly in a believable way;
- a local term is unfamiliar to an international reader but understandable in context;
- an editor personally prefers another ending.

Protect deliberate artistic choices. Explain rather than erase them when they may be misunderstood.

## 34. Handling uncertainty

Use these labels consistently in all editorial and research logs:

- **VERIFIED:** supported by the manuscript or an authoritative source.
- **STRONGLY SUPPORTED:** multiple reliable indicators, but a small uncertainty remains.
- **PLAUSIBLE:** reasonable but not verified.
- **AMBIGUOUS:** more than one interpretation is possible.
- **CONTRADICTED:** conflicts with another established fact or a reliable source.
- **UNVERIFIED:** evidence is insufficient.
- **AUTHOR DECISION REQUIRED:** the issue affects core creative intent or cannot be safely decided by the editor.

Never convert ambiguity into certainty by confident wording.

# PART VI — FINAL SUCCESS STANDARD

A successful revision is not the longest version, the most dramatic version or the version with the highest self-awarded score. It is the version in which:

- Shanta's story is emotionally credible from the first page to the last;
- the room and its people feel specific, inhabited and irreplaceable;
- the paper trail is intricate but understandable;
- the mystery develops through fair evidence and meaningful reversals;
- the supporting characters are individual human beings;
- the prose retains its literary identity while becoming more controlled;
- the legal/procedural elements withstand responsible scrutiny;
- the ending fulfils the book's promises without pretending that every harm can be repaired;
- every sentence, scene and chapter earns its place;
- the final reader and print files are clean, consistent and professionally presented.

The desired result is a novel that readers remember for its people, its world and its moral force—not because an editor has labelled it 9.8/10.

**Final instruction:** Do the full work. Read the whole manuscript. Verify before changing. Revise with restraint. Preserve the author's voice. Keep every material change traceable. Never invent, fake, conceal or inflate. Deliver only files and reports that have actually been completed and checked.

**Neither review supersedes the manuscript.** The v4 manuscript and any subsequently verified authoritative editable source remain the primary evidence. Every recommendation must be tested against the complete text, page images where relevant, chronology, character knowledge, document/evidence chain and legal/procedural research.

Do not paste the two reviews into the workflow as competing command lists. Reconcile them into one evidence-led issue register, resolve contradictions, rank the issues by severity and dependencies, and then revise.

## 27. Mandatory review-reconciliation protocol

Before making any change derived from either review, create `TWO_REVIEW_RECONCILIATION.md` and a machine-readable or tabular issue ledger.

For every individual recommendation, record:

1. Review source: Review A or Review B.
2. Exact recommendation, faithfully summarised or quoted in a short excerpt.
3. Manuscript locator: chapter, page in the identified edition, and a unique text anchor or quotation.
4. Evidence status:
   - `VERIFIED_DEFECT` — directly demonstrated by the authoritative source.
   - `PROBABLE_DEFECT` — strong evidence, but requires another check.
   - `INTERPRETIVE_CONCERN` — a plausible reader/editor response, not an objective error.
   - `OPTIONAL_ENHANCEMENT` — a possible improvement that must earn its place.
   - `UNSUPPORTED` — the manuscript/reliable evidence does not substantiate the claim.
   - `CONTRADICTED` — the claim conflicts with the manuscript or verified evidence.
   - `UNRESOLVED` — insufficient evidence; do not silently decide.
5. Severity: critical, major, moderate or minor.
6. Proposed remedy and the least disruptive viable alternative.
7. Downstream dependencies: chapters, dates, objects, character knowledge, evidence, legal steps, production files.
8. Risk of changing authorial intent, plot causality, character motivation or thematic meaning.
9. Approval status: safe editorial correction, author approval required, or do not implement.
10. Verification evidence after implementation.

## 28. Review A — operational findings to investigate

### 28.1 Chronology and calendar integrity

Build one master chronology that includes both the novel's narrated event chronology and the order in which the reader learns information. Do not stop the timeline at September 2027: the v4 ending material includes a December sitting. The chronology must extend through the last dated event in the authoritative manuscript, including December 2027 if that date remains in the chosen source.

Audit every explicit and implied date against:

- day of week;
- public holidays and relevant institutional working days;
- court, police, office and society schedules;
- travel time and physical movement;
- character age and elapsed time;
- document creation, signing, notarisation, filing, discovery and custody;
- seasonal/weather references;
- references such as "yesterday", "last week", "three months ago" and "fifty years".

Do not invent an event, alibi, biometric camp, public programme, holiday exception or institutional opening merely to make a proposed date work. In particular, any proposed April 14, 2026 Ambedkar Jayanti alibi or event must be independently verified and must fit the setting and story. A calendar fact alone does not establish that a fictional event occurred.

**Known v4 issue to re-check against the authoritative source:** the Prologue identifies March 28 while nearby seasonal references appear to point toward late May. Determine whether the discrepancy is real, whether the references describe the same time and place, and which date is authoritative. Do not mechanically change the Prologue date without tracing every dependent passage.

### 28.2 The nail / fifty-year continuity

Preserve the opening prediction that the nail would be "good for fifty years" if it is an intentional prediction or motif. The apparent continuity problem is in later retrospective statements: v4 includes Shanta saying that the fifty years have passed and another statement that the nail has held for fifty years, while the story's 1982–2026 interval is approximately 44 years.

Check the exact dates and wording in the authoritative manuscript. Correct only the later elapsed-time claims if the timeline confirms the discrepancy. Do not globally delete every occurrence of "fifty years"; doing so could destroy the original prediction and its intended meaning. Record every occurrence and classify it by function before editing.

### 28.3 Physical geography and scene blocking

Reconstruct the physical space of every important scene. Check stairs, landings, doors, windows, corridors, rooms, entrances, courthouse approaches and movement between locations. A character must be able to reach, see, hear, carry, open, conceal or retrieve what the prose says they do.

**Known v4 issue to re-check:** Chapter 38 appears to create a mismatch between the courthouse steps and a wooden-stair description. Inspect the actual passage and the surrounding page image. Determine whether the issue is a physical contradiction, an extraction artefact, or an intentional description of a different stairway. Do not silently replace a location detail based only on extracted text.

### 28.4 Corrupted seams, joined words and typographic defects

Run a complete copyediting and text-integrity pass for:

- joined words and missing spaces;
- missing or duplicated words;
- broken paragraph joins;
- malformed quotation marks;
- inconsistent apostrophes;
- accidental punctuation duplication;
- stray glyphs and encoding substitutions;
- paragraph fragments caused by editing or PDF conversion.

**Known v4 issue to re-check:** Chapter 39 contains an apparent joined-word seam rendered as "TheMisal boys". Verify against the editable source and page image before correcting it. Search for comparable seams throughout the manuscript; do not assume this is the only occurrence.

Automated checks are aids, not authorities. Correct regex escaping and test patterns on a controlled sample before using them. A literal two-period sequence and a regular expression containing two wildcard dots are not the same test. Preserve valid ellipses and intentional punctuation. Do not run broad replacements that can damage dialogue, abbreviations or literary rhythm.

### 28.5 Dialogue and voice editing

Review the proposed dialogue changes character by character and scene by scene. Do not adopt a quota such as rewriting 35–40% of supporting-character dialogue. A percentage target is arbitrary and can flatten distinctive speech.

Create a voice guide for each significant character, considering:

- vocabulary and register;
- sentence length and rhythm;
- education, profession and practical knowledge;
- regional, linguistic and generational influence where supported;
- humour, evasions, silences and emotional defences;
- preferred metaphors or lack of metaphor;
- how the character behaves under pressure;
- what the character would plausibly know;
- what the character would never naturally say.

Revise only dialogue that is interchangeable, expository, implausibly polished, repetitive or inconsistent with the speaker. Preserve productive ambiguity, silence, ordinary speech and individual verbal habits. Do not make every supporting character witty, quotable or philosophically articulate.

### 28.6 Ambient details and scene atmosphere

Do not delete every ambient line simply to accelerate pacing. The building's routines, sounds, meals, weather, corridors and ordinary objects may carry atmosphere, social observation, characterisation or thematic function.

For each candidate cut, identify what it contributes and what would be lost. Cut, compress or relocate only when the detail repeats an already-established effect, interrupts a scene's pressure without compensation, or does no meaningful work. Preserve quiet scenes when their emotional or evidentiary function is real.

### 28.7 Legal and procedural setbacks

Test whether the procedural sequence contains credible friction, delays, limitations, costs and setbacks. Do not invent a judicial defeat, adverse order, procedural obstacle or legal consequence simply because a review recommends a stronger setback. First map the existing obstacles and distinguish what the characters experience from what the law or institution actually permits.

For every legal or procedural change:

- identify the exact legal proposition;
- verify the applicable jurisdiction, date and institutional context;
- use reliable primary or authoritative sources where possible;
- distinguish law, practice, custom, strategy and fictional invention;
- obtain qualified legal review for material legal claims where necessary;
- keep uncertainty visible in the research register.

Do not insert a statute, circular, section number, case, authority or legal phrase because it sounds plausible. In particular, do not assume that a cooperative-housing-society directive or Section 79A Maharashtra framework necessarily governs this tenancy/redevelopment fact pattern. Establish applicability before using it.

## 29. Review B — literary-development findings to investigate

### 29.1 Antagonist competence without omnipotence

Test whether the antagonist or opposing network behaves like a credible, adaptive operator rather than a passive plot device. Ask:

- What does the opposing side want at each stage?
- What do they know, and how did they learn it?
- Which records, intermediaries, institutions or relationships can they realistically influence?
- What risks do they understand?
- What mistakes, constraints, blind spots and internal disagreements limit them?
- Why do they fail to anticipate or prevent particular discoveries?
- What evidence links their actions to the outcome?

Make the antagonist more credible through demonstrated methods, incentives, information flow and operational constraints—not by simply making them more powerful, more connected or more omniscient.

Investigate the review's questions about the retained archive, typewriter clues and the opposing side's handling of documents. Do not force all clues into a single conspiracy mechanism unless the manuscript supports that connection.

**Important machine-specific continuity safeguard:** the review material distinguishes the limping lowercase `g` associated with Sawant's typewriter from the machine used by Tambe & Sons. Preserve that distinction if confirmed by the source. Do not merge separate machines, owners, locations or document-production processes for the sake of simplifying the clue.

A passage describing a printing-shop boy receiving "typing matter" or a draft already typed on a pendrive needs careful interpretation. A mechanical typewriter cannot store a digital file, but the wording may refer to a digitally prepared document or to the transfer of content rather than to a mechanical typewriter. Trace the exact sequence, device, file, operator and medium before changing it. Do not convert an ambiguity into a false technical correction.

### 29.2 Distinct supporting-character voices

Use the voice-guide method in §28.5. Review B's concern is not a demand for theatrical accents or exaggerated dialect. The goal is that readers can often identify a speaker from the wording, rhythm, priorities and evasions even without a dialogue tag.

Do not manufacture linguistic traits, caste/class markers, regional stereotypes, educational backgrounds or biographical facts. Ground each voice in the character already established by the novel.

### 29.3 Abhijit's generational and emotional conflict

Treat this as an existing arc to deepen, not a blank space to fill with a newly invented conflict.

The v4 text already establishes Abhijit's desire for a new flat "with a lift", his financial contribution/signature on a membership-related form, and Shanta's recognition that he was used. The later letter also distinguishes his signature from his full personhood and identifies the manipulator behind the form. Re-read the relevant scenes in the authoritative manuscript before deciding what is missing.

Assess whether the current arc fully communicates:

- Abhijit's desire for security, status, comfort or independence, to the extent the text supports those motives;
- the practical and emotional pressures behind his choices;
- his responsibility without reducing him to a villain;
- Shanta's hurt, disappointment and continuing maternal attachment;
- the difference between a signature, an action, an intention and a person's entire identity;
- whether the letter and later scenes produce a credible emotional change.

Do not add a communal-toilet embarrassment motive, class shame, financial motive or other specific psychology unless the manuscript or author confirms it. The review's proposed interpretation is a hypothesis to test, not a fact to insert.

### 29.4 Procedural pacing and repetition

Review the procedural middle—especially the chapters identified by the reviewers, including the Ch.14–24 range—as a sequence of dramatic turns, not merely a sequence of documents, meetings and explanations.

For every procedural scene, identify:

- the immediate objective;
- the obstacle;
- the new fact or changed understanding;
- the decision or cost;
- the effect on the next scene;
- whether the reader's understanding or the balance of power changes.

Where several scenes perform the same function, consider merging, compressing, reordering or differentiating them. Do not remove a procedural step that is necessary to make the evidence chain understandable or legally plausible. Do not compress the process until the reader can no longer follow who created, possessed, altered, authenticated, submitted or discovered a document.

Increase tension through consequence, uncertainty, time pressure, exposure, cost and conflicting incentives—not through arbitrary cliffhangers or artificial setbacks.

### 29.5 The ending: preserve its existing moral complexity

Do not assume the ending is cleanly celebratory or that it needs a newly added bittersweet ending. The v4 ending already appears to leave important consequences unresolved: the builder is not included in the charge-sheet, company liability remains in other files and other years, and the enforcement struggle is described as longer than the immediate victory.

Re-read the final chapters and assess what is actually resolved, what remains unresolved, who bears the cost, and what Shanta's final emotional state communicates. Preserve this existing complexity if it is working.

Do not add a fresh tragedy, new legal defeat, sentimental reconciliation or bleak coda merely to make the ending "more bittersweet". Strengthen the ending only where the text demonstrates a genuine gap between the novel's promises and its final emotional or practical consequences.
